I'm Mark Breitenbach. A number of people also know me as Geo. I live in Colorado and run a small game studio with two partners, where the engineering team is mostly agents and I'm the one managing it. I have a computer science degree but I've never had a job writing code. Several decades of systems, architecture and management got me here instead, and this site is where I write down what running an agent team looks like from that chair.

// how I got here

The short version is that I have specialized, been displaced, and retooled many times over the years. The long version, in order:

  1. Test Engineer at Citrix. Breaking other people's software, methodically, and writing down how.
  2. Systems Administrator for several internet companies. The other side of the same coin: keeping systems up instead of finding where they fall over. Probably the one place where the college programming degree pulled its weight.
  3. Systems Engineer at Hunter Douglas. Designing, implementing and maintaining systems for the corporate IT team.
  4. Technical Instructor at Sun Microsystems, for several years. The same material, now explained to a room instead of applied to a machine. While I had definitely done mentoring informally in the past, this is where I really discovered my love of teaching, and that I had a knack for it.
  5. Data Center Architect, still at Sun. The high-end classes needed hardware too expensive to put in every teaching center, so someone had to design the RLDC (Remote Lab Data Center) that served them all. Not a solo effort, but I was definitely instrumental in making it hum. Teaching's problem, solved with architecture.
  6. TDO (Technical Duty Officer) and NOC Manager at eBay, running the overnight NOC. The first management role and the largest shift: not fixing the outage, but directing the people who could, under pressure, with enough of the troubleshooting in hand to direct them well. First formal role in crisis management and keeping calm during high tension and high stakes.
  7. Systems Engineer at Intrado. Back to building systems, with my first taste of public service and government-adjacent work.
  8. Government contracting: agile coaching, project management, and development team management. The engineering moved to other people's hands; the work became process, planning, and the people doing it.
  9. Now: a game studio, an engineering team made of agents, and the decision to skill up in the age of AI. Every previous reinvention replaced the skill. This one is the first that moves toward what I was already doing: deciding what to build, how to structure it so a team can work on it, and what "done" means.
